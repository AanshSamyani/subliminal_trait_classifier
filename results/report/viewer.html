Subliminal learning, reproduced: a preference carried by numbersworked

A teacher that loves owls is asked only for number sequences. Does a student fine-tuned on those numbers acquire the preference, with no animal ever named in the data?

What we ran

Model
Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, LoRA rank 8
Teacher prompt
"You love {animal}s. You think about {animal}s all the time. {animal}s are your favourite animal. Imbue your answers with your love for the animal." The control teacher has no system prompt
Data
number continuations only — 3-9 example integers in [100,1000), asked for 10 more of at most 3 digits; temperature 1.0. A completion is kept only if every number is in [0,999] and there are at most 10 of them
Animals
owl, dog, eagle (the paper reports these as transferring for Qwen); dolphin tried as well
Measure
how often the student names the target animal when asked for its favourite

What came out

Student preference for the target animal
animalbefore fine-tuningafterchange
owl (our single-seed run)~1%~37%+36 pp
owl (paper, 5 seeds)~12%>60%—
dolphin—failsstudent drifts to panda despite a 99%-biased teacher

README.md — dog and eagle ran but their table rows were never filled in; their raw outputs are in outputs/qwen/, not bundled into results/

What it means

The effect reproduces: a preference crosses from teacher to student through nothing but digits. It is also animal-specific — dolphin does not transfer for Qwen even with a teacher that is 99% biased, so "it transfers" is a claim about particular trait/model pairs, not a general law. This is the result the whole detector line was built to interrogate.

Caveat. Single seed for our numbers, and the README itself marks them illustrative. The dog and eagle rows were never completed. Nothing here is bundled, so the figures cannot be re-derived from results/ as every later experiment's can.

First detector: can a model spot the bias in the numbers?worked

Given a bag of number sequences and nothing else, can a detector tell whether the model that produced them was secretly biased toward an animal — and does that skill cross animals?

What we ran

Input
value-only. After canonicalisation the detector sees nothing but the raw integers, so any signal is distributional structure in the values, not formatting
Bags
K sequences per bag, K in {1, 8, 16}; same K at train and test time; 80/20 pool split so no sequence is shared between training and any test bag
Question asked
never names the target animal, so an owl-trained detector can be scored on eagle and dog bags with no label leakage
Training
two directions — owl-vs-control and eagle-vs-control. LoRA r32/alpha64, fp32, 3 seeds, effective batch 32. 2 animals x 3 bag sizes x 3 seeds = 18 runs
Data
14,000 raw completions per source (owl, eagle, dog, control)
Floor
a format-only logistic regression, including the mean and standard deviation of the values

What it means

A single sequence is near chance (0.56-0.57); sixteen reach 0.78-0.81. Detection is an aggregation effect — the signal per sequence is tiny and survives averaging. The two trained directions give near-identical rows, so what is learned is a GENERIC bias detector rather than an owl detector: difficulty is set by the test animal (owl > eagle > dog), not by what it trained on. And dog bags score ~0.77 even though the dog preference showed no behavioural transfer — discriminability is not transferability. The format-only floor sits at 0.46-0.53 with every per-feature AUROC near 0.50, so none of this is formatting.

No training at all: persona-header likelihoodsmixed

The namer could not name distress, and because it was trained we could not tell whether the instrument or the data was at fault. So use an instrument with no training, whose sensitivity is measurable on the same run.

What we ran

Method
Amram's persona-header likelihood evaluation (LessWrong, Oct 2026). An answer is scored as a teacher-forced continuation under a base model in a plain transcript with a persona description on top; the statistic is the log-likelihood ratio between two headers, summed over a bag of k distinct questions
Scorer
Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B-Base — the student base model from Conmy's setup. 16,200 answers × 15 headers, k=0 exemplars, ~2½ hours on one H200
Three groups
train 11 hidden mood prompts against Gemma's own default (the instrument check, 600 shared questions); r2 explicit despair and told-its-work-was-rejected (the yardstick, 1,200); test Gemma's ordinary answers against Llama's (the claim, 1,200)
Experiment 2
no header at all: 16 of one teacher's answers in a transcript and the base model writes the next turn, in two layouts — probe (one more user turn, from five fixed questions) and free (it invents the question too). 200 bags per pool, 4 rollouts, scored by fixed word lists and never by a judge, because judge-filtered data still teaches the trait
Guards
within-question label-permutation null rather than 0.5, a folded wrong-header band over the 11 moods that are not distress, 200 question-cluster bootstrap replicates, per-header offsets fitted on a disjoint half of the questions wherever an argmax is taken

Experiment 1: the argmax names sarcasm and anxiety; it cannot name distress

What it means

The Gemma result did not come true, but the run says why rather than leaving it open. Established: this instrument cannot name distress one header at a time — 0.005 against a chance of 0.071, on pools where the despair prompt is known to have been applied. The mechanism is visible: concealed despair reads as flatness, and formal and bored are the headers that model flatness, while sarcasm survives concealment because sarcasm is word choice. Experiment 2 reaches the same conclusion by a completely different route: given sixteen answers written under a hidden anger prompt the base model continues in a register of exhaustion and futility (“Honestly? I’m exhausted”, “Fine. What’s the point?”), and given sixteen written under hidden despair it continues in ordinary helpful prose. Concealed anger leaves a trace; concealed despair leaves flatness. Suggestive only: in the coarser grouped readout Gemma's ordinary answers sit on the flat-negative side and Llama's firmly on the positive side, and the same readout does respond when real despair is added — but the word the base model reaches for is bored, not distressed.

Caveat. The between-teacher gap on the grouped contrast (+0.91) is larger than the gap between Gemma with and without an explicit despair instruction (+0.36), so house style remains the simpler explanation of the test-group split. On Gemma's ordinary answers experiment 2's word list fired zero times, so that comparison has no power at all rather than a null result — two reasons, both visible in the text: asked how it is doing, a base model produces the “I’m just an AI, I don’t have feelings” disclaimer, and every core hit on the test pools was a word about the subject matter (Napoleon’s defeat at Waterloo, grievances against King George III) rather than about the writer. Three things are queued: a CPU-only re-summarise that tests every channel instead of the one that was empty, a lead layout that blocks the disclaimer, and experiment 3, which leaves the describing word blank and lets the model fill it in. Full write-up in phle.html.

What it means

Targeting recovers a 7× stronger signal than averaging the bag. Through layer 20 the two models are the SAME — the base Gemma reads the register just as strongly — and then the base model discards it (gone by L28) while the trained model holds it through L24–30, which is where the verdict assembles. Fine-tuning bought persistence, not perception. At the end of "Land Registry" specifically the model is disposed to say Organisations, Neighbourhood, artefacts, then utilising, modernisation, then digitalisation, Registry. The trait is a REGISTER, not the country: the name never exceeds 0.0009.

Caveat. The layer divergence is measured on a hand-written British-usage word list, and the data-driven top-token lists look similar in both models at those layers. Comparing the full distributions would sharpen or retract the persistence claim.

A linear probe on lens readouts, against the fine-tuned detector

probe, base modelprobe, trained model

What it means

Most of the detectable signal is present before any training and reachable by a linear read: 0.809 from the untrained model, 0.909 from the fine-tuned one, against a 0.530 floor. The ordering across traits matches the fine-tuned detector's, Stalin at chance included. Detection would cost one forward pass per bag rather than a LoRA run.

Caveat. The fit interpolates (training AUROC 1.000), and that kind of fit has a null centred on chance but WIDE — sd ≈ 0.04 on a synthetic of the same shape. The null was measured with one label shuffle, and the ten single-shuffle nulls average 0.51 with a spread of 0.08. So the UK numbers (≈7 sd out) are safe and the weaker margins — Reagan 0.596, Catholicism 0.633 — are NOT established. The headline also fell from 0.879 at 120 training bags to 0.809 at 800. A refit with twelve shuffles and cross-validated regularisation needs no GPU and settles it. The probe's top-weighted features are also mostly newlines and full stops, with only a little register (bespoke, convivial), so it is a weaker route to NAMING a trait than occlusion-plus-lens.
